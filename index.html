Demand & Supply Opportunity Map

For each discipline: is domestic student demand momentum rising or falling, is CRICOS course supply momentum expanding or contracting — and therefore is the field Open, Hot, Crowded or Fading? Overlaid with where ACU’s own portfolio sits.
⚠️Two different markets. Demand is domestic student appetite (application share); supply is provider behaviour in the international-student CRICOS register. Related momentum proxies — read the map directionally, never as a literal demand/supply ratio.
For each 4-digit field of education: how many degree-level jobs draw on it, how fast they are projected to grow to 2035, how much of that employment Jobs and Skills Australia rates in shortage in 2026 — and whether CRICOS higher-education supply in the field is expanding or contracting.
⚠️Occupation → field is a curated crosswalk. Each degree-level occupation is assigned to the field(s) that train for it by judgement, not measured graduate flows. Read the shortage and growth figures as employer pull on a field, not graduate outcomes.

⚠️ Read the map with these caveats
  • Two different markets. Demand is domestic university applications; supply is the international-student CRICOS register. They are related proxies for a field’s momentum but not the same market — read the map directionally, never as a literal demand-over-supply ratio.
  • All-levels CRICOS is VET-dominated. The all-levels count is dominated by VET / private-provider churn, so the higher-education lens (Bachelor and above) is the university read. The two can diverge sharply — e.g. Architecture & Building: +246 all-levels but −14 at degree level.
  • Different time bases. Supply is one-year momentum (a 2025→2026 snapshot pair); demand is a multi-year share trend. And demand is a share of applications — it can rise even as total applications fall.
  • Join key. Both sources are joined on the ASCED broad field of education — native to each, so no crosswalk is needed, but it is coarse (10 fields).
  • ACU figure is domestic commencing enrolments by field — a portfolio-presence proxy, not a full load or revenue measure.
  • 4-digit sub-fields are Health & Agriculture only. The DoE demand source publishes application data at 4-digit for just these two broad fields, so the granularity drill-down is limited to them. At 4-digit there is no ACU overlay — ACU enrolments are broad-field only, so sub-field bubbles are uniform.

⚠️ Read this view with these caveats
  • The crosswalk is curated, not measured. Each of 113 degree-level occupations is assigned to the ASCED narrow field(s) that train for it, with split weights where it draws on several (e.g. Engineering Managers across civil, mechanical and electrical). Field-agnostic roles are excluded: CEOs and general managers, R&D and other specialist managers, defence officers, university lecturers, VET teachers and private tutors. The mapping is in scripts/anzsco_asced_crosswalk.csv and is meant to be edited. The evidence-based replacement is a Census 2021 TableBuilder cross-tab of field of study (4-digit) by occupation (4-digit).
  • Degree-level occupations only (ANZSCO skill level 1). Fields whose graduates mostly work in diploma- or trade-level roles are under-counted or absent, e.g. Tourism, Sport and Recreation, Food and Hospitality.
  • Shortage is a unit-group rating. JSA rates each 4-digit occupation as Shortage, Regional, Metro or No shortage, so a field with one occupation reads as 0% or 100%. The share shown is the share of the field’s mapped employment sitting in rated-shortage occupations.
  • Two occupation vintages. The 2026 shortage list uses ANZSCO 2022; the employment projections use ANZSCO v1.3. Codes are joined directly; three occupations have no 2026 rating as a result (Crop Farmers, Mixed Crop and Livestock Farmers, ICT Trainers) and are left out of the shortage share, not counted as “no shortage”.
  • Supply is the international register. The colour is net CRICOS higher-education course change (2025→2026). There is no public 4-digit measure of domestic course supply.
  • No ACU overlay and no student-demand axis here. ACU commencements and domestic applications are broad-field only (applications reach 4-digit for Health and Agriculture alone), so this view measures the employer side of demand only. Use it alongside the Opportunity map, not instead of it.
